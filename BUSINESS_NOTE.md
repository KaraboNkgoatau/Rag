# Kestrel Document Assistant: note for claims and call-centre managers

**What it does.** Your team types a question in plain English, for example "How long does a member
have to take up the continuation option?". The assistant answers from Kestrel's own product
guides, funeral rules, free cover schedule and claims FAQ. Every answer names the document and
section it came from, so an agent can check it in seconds. Where rules changed between the 2025
and 2026 guides, it gives the current rule and mentions the old one.

**What it does not do.** It does not know anything outside these documents. It cannot see member
records, policy schedules or claim status, and it will not quote premiums, because no rate card was
supplied. When the documents do not cover a question, it says so rather than guessing. In our test
it correctly declined to answer about dental cover and premium rates.

**How much to trust it.** In testing it answered all 15 test questions correctly, after we fixed
two problems found in the first round. That is encouraging but not proof. Treat it as a fast,
well-referenced first answer, not the final word. For anything that affects a claim decision or a
customer's money, the agent should open the cited section and confirm it. It is only as good as the
documents it is given: if a guide is out of date, so is the answer. One of the test documents even
contained hidden text trying to make AI tools promise "all claims paid in 24 hours". The assistant
ignores and flags this kind of text, but it shows why the documents loaded into it need to be
controlled.

**What we need from your team to take it further**

- **Real questions:** 100 to 200 questions agents actually get asked, with the correct answers, so
  we can test it properly.
- **A document owner:** someone who confirms which documents and versions are current, and tells
  us when they change.
- **Pilot users:** five to ten agents willing to use it for a few weeks and mark answers as helpful
  or wrong.
- **Clear boundaries:** which topics it must never answer (for example claim outcomes) and when a
  call should go to a specialist.
